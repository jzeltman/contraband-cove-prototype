# Storage and export playtest · 0.5.0

This build starts a fresh prototype save. No migration is intended.

1. Start a shift: the clock shows **1:00**. Judge cloth and supplies separately. Each correct lot pays 20 coins and occupies one yard space. Inspecting and switching lots still preserve evidence.
2. Try the next ship. With two of the three spaces occupied, its two-lot shipment must wait. Open **Yard** in the header: visible filled/empty spaces show capacity. Tap an import to deliver it; held cargo goes to secure custody. Manual delivery is free.
3. Buy a warehouse expansion for 60 coins: three spaces become six. The next expansion costs 120 and opens nine spaces, including the annex. Does space feel worth buying?
4. Buy **Longer shifts** for 60 coins. The current timer must stay unchanged; the next shift starts at 1:30. The second upgrade costs 120 and grants 2:00 on subsequent shifts.
5. Hire a **porter** for 80. Watch an import leave after eight active seconds. A donkey costs 80 and cuts delivery time to four seconds; a cart costs 120 and cuts it to two. Porters never remove exports. Manual delivery remains available.
6. Wait 20 active seconds for an island delivery. Open the yard and unload it into spare space. Waiting producers are capped at two; they do not crowd out reserved inbound cargo.
7. Store two exports if space permits. Meet an export captain. Read the goods and destination, then select matching cargo. Try an import or a mismatched destination: it must stay in storage and explain the mismatch without a fine. Load every order line, then dispatch for 25 coins per lot, paid once.
8. Let time expire during export loading. No further cargo can be loaded. Begin the next shift, acknowledge the rules, return to the yard and resume the same order. Already-loaded cargo and payment state must persist.
9. Let time expire during inspection. Only the selected lot gets overtime; remaining lots keep their progress. Manual deliveries are available after closing so full storage cannot trap you.
10. Pause, view a reference, open the map and background the app. Clock, porter and Mara stop together. Yard work during an open shift consumes time. No offline income.
11. Hire Mara, train her, and check a combined seal/weight case as in the earlier build. Storage must not change evidence requirements or per-lot verdicts.
12. Reload during partial delivery/loading. Load offline after one online visit. Test the installed iPhone safe areas, Android, text zoom, and Help → Apply downloaded update.

Questions: Is one minute exciting or too rushed? Does storage create interesting choices or too much tapping? Which first purchase feels useful: time, warehouse, Mara or porter? Does automatic delivery feel visible and worthwhile? Do exports feel different from customs inspection? Is matching goods/destination too easy? Would you play another shift?

Automated checks establish behavior, not enjoyment. Physical-device and fun feedback remain with the owner in GitHub #12. Prototype tuning values are deliberately easy to change.
